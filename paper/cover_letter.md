Dear Editors of Research in Economics,

Please consider the manuscript “Equilibrium Scope and Welfare in History-Based
Price Discrimination: A Reassessment of Gehrig, Shy, and Stenbacka (2011)” for
publication in Research in Economics.

The manuscript provides an independent theoretical reassessment of Thomas
Gehrig, Oz Shy, and Rune Stenbacka (2011), “History-based Price Discrimination
and Entry in Markets with Switching Costs: A Welfare Analysis,” European
Economic Review 55(5), 732–739. It establishes two substantive corrections.

First, it derives exact parameter conditions under which the paper's displayed
history-based and uniform-pricing profiles are global Nash equilibria. Strict
interiority of the displayed cohort shares is not sufficient because a firm
charging a common price across cohorts can profitably cross an active-set
boundary and abandon one cohort.

Second, direct integration of primitive consumer utilities produces a
consumer-surplus difference with a factor of three missing from the complete
author draft. On the common certified domain, this correction reverses the
displayed-equilibrium welfare ranking: uniform pricing yields higher total
welfare, even though history-based pricing improves spatial matching, because
the induced increase in switching-cost losses is larger.

The contribution is intentionally compact and source-specific. The manuscript
does not claim uniqueness, a full equilibrium correspondence outside the
certified domains, or a general welfare ranking for history-based or
personalized pricing. Independent clean-room computation and targeted Lean
verification are supplied as supporting reproducibility materials rather than
as the economic contribution.
