## Abstract

This paper reassesses the welfare comparison in Gehrig, Shy, and Stenbacka
(2011), a Hotelling model of history-based price discrimination with switching
costs. We establish two corrections. First, after cohort demands are globally
clipped to the unit interval, the displayed interior price formulas need not be
global Nash equilibria: a common-price firm can profitably cross an active-set
boundary and abandon one cohort. We derive exact global-validity regions for
the displayed history-based and uniform-pricing profiles and show that the
uniform-pricing region is nested inside the history-based-pricing region.
Second, direct integration of primitive consumer utilities yields a
consumer-surplus difference three times the expression reported in the complete
author draft. On the common validity domain, uniform pricing raises consumer
surplus by 3 theta(1-theta) sigma^2/(16 tau), reduces incumbent profit by
theta(1-theta) sigma^2/(8 tau), and leaves entrant profit unchanged. Total
welfare is therefore higher under the displayed uniform-pricing equilibrium by
theta(1-theta) sigma^2/(16 tau). The result is deliberately source-specific: we
do not claim uniqueness, nonexistence of equilibrium outside these regions, or
a general welfare ranking across pricing regimes.
