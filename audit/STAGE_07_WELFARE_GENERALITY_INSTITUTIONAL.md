## Canonical verdict

`GO — SUBSTANTIVE WELFARE CORRECTION; GENERALITY RESTRICTED TO THE GSS CLASS`

Stage 7 does not add a new equilibrium theorem. It identifies the real-resource

## 1. Welfare accounting from primitives

The Stage-4A certified displayed equilibria satisfy

`CS^u-CS^d = 3 theta(1-theta)sigma^2/(16 tau)`,

`pi_A^d-pi_A^u = theta(1-theta)sigma^2/(8 tau)`,

`pi_B^d-pi_B^u = 0`,

and therefore

`W^d-W^u = -theta(1-theta)sigma^2/(16 tau)`

on `D_compare=D_U`.

This is not a transfer-only welfare comparison.

## 2. Allocation decomposition

The old-consumer incumbent share changes by

`x_o^d-x_o^u = - theta sigma/(4 tau)`.

Thus HBP sends **more old consumers to entrant B** by

`theta sigma/(4 tau)`.

The new-consumer incumbent share changes by

`x_n^d-x_n^u = (1-theta) sigma/(4 tau)`.

Thus HBP sends **more new consumers to incumbent A** by

`(1-theta) sigma/(4 tau)`.

The weighted aggregate incumbent share remains unchanged:

`m_A^d=m_A^u`.

Hence HBP changes the **composition** of each firm's customer base without
changing aggregate firm quantities.

Because aggregate firm shares are unchanged, total production-cost expenditure
is identical across the displayed regimes even when `c_A != c_B`.

## 3. Price decomposition

Relative to uniform pricing:

`p_A^d-p_A^u = theta sigma/2` for old consumers,

`q_A^d-p_A^u = -(1-theta) sigma/2` for new consumers,

and

`p_B^d-p_B^u=0`.

Thus the incumbent uses HBP to tilt its price schedule upward for old consumers
and downward for new consumers, while the entrant's displayed common price is
unchanged.

This produces a sharp empirical/comparative prediction inside the model:
customer composition changes even though the entrant price and total firm
market shares do not.

## 4. Real-resource welfare decomposition

For a cohort whose cutoff is `x`, Hotelling transportation cost is

`tau[x^2-x+1/2]`.

Direct substitution of the certified displayed allocations gives

`TC^d-TC^u = -3 theta(1-theta)sigma^2/(16 tau)`.

So HBP **reduces transportation mismatch**.

Old consumers buying from B bear the switching cost. Direct substitution gives

`SC^d-SC^u = theta(1-theta)sigma^2/(4 tau)`.

So HBP **raises switching-cost losses** by

`4 theta(1-theta)sigma^2/(16 tau)`.

Combining the two:

`(TC^d+SC^d)-(TC^u+SC^u)
  = theta(1-theta)sigma^2/(16 tau)`.

Therefore HBP's transportation-cost improvement is more than offset by the
additional switching cost. Total real resource cost is higher under HBP by
exactly the amount of the welfare loss.

Equivalently:

`W^d-W^u
 = -[(TC^d+SC^d)-(TC^u+SC^u)]`.

This decomposition is the economically substantive interpretation of P4.

`code/stage07_welfare_decomposition.py`.

## 5. Distributional decomposition

The incumbent's HBP profit gain is

`theta(1-theta)sigma^2/(8 tau)`.

The consumer loss from HBP is

`3 theta(1-theta)sigma^2/(16 tau)`.

Hence

`(incumbent profit gain)/(consumer loss)=2/3`.

The entrant's displayed equilibrium profit is unchanged.

Thus two thirds of the consumer loss is transferred into incumbent operating
profit and one third remains as the net social loss.

This directly pinpoints the error in the source welfare headline: the incumbent
profit gain does **not** exceed the consumer loss.

## 6. Private versus social ranking

On the strict certified common domain with
`0<theta<1, sigma>0, tau>0`:

- incumbent A strictly prefers HBP in operating profit;
- entrant B is indifferent in operating profit between the displayed regimes;
- consumers strictly prefer uniform pricing;
- total welfare strictly prefers uniform pricing.

This is a genuine private/social conflict inside the model.

However, the pricing regime is not an endogenous adoption or regulatory choice
in the source game. Therefore the project must **not** infer:

- over-adoption of HBP by firms;
- the optimality of banning HBP;
- the optimal regulatory instrument;
- implementation or enforcement effects.

Those questions require a regime-choice/policy stage absent from GSS (2011).

Maximum defensible wording:

> Conditional on the displayed equilibria being valid, the incumbent's private
> gain from HBP is smaller than the consumer loss, and the residual difference
> is a real-resource welfare loss generated by additional switching.

## 7. Institutional validation of core primitives

Institutional evidence is used only to support the plausibility of the
primitives, not to validate the exact GSS calibration or welfare coefficient.

### A. History/data-based price differentiation

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission's surveillance-pricing work reports that
consumer characteristics and behavior, including location, browsing patterns
and shopping history, can be used to tailor prices or promotions.

Primary sources:

  proposed enforcement-policy statement regarding personalized pricing:
  https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/08/ftc-seeks-comment-enforcement-policy-statement-regarding-personalized-pricing

Implication for GSS:
firms' ability to condition commercial terms on customer history/type is
institutionally plausible.

Not established:
the exact one-sided information asymmetry between incumbent and entrant assumed
by GSS.

### B. Tenure / existing-versus-new customer pricing

The UK Financial Conduct Authority documented "price walking" in home and motor
insurance and adopted rules requiring renewal prices not to exceed equivalent
new-business prices. Its later evaluation reports that the reforms reduced
price walking.

Primary sources:

- FCA, 28 May 2021,
  *FCA confirms measures to protect customers from the loyalty penalty*:
  https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-confirms-measures-protect-customers-loyalty-penalty-home-motor-insurance-markets
- FCA Handbook, ICOBS 6B.2:
  https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/icobs6b/icobs6bs2
- FCA 2025 evaluation of General Insurance Pricing Practices remedies:
  https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/corporate/ep25-2.pdf

Implication for GSS:
existing/new customer status can be economically relevant to pricing.

Not established:
the exact GSS Hotelling structure or the entrant's restricted discrimination
technology.

### C. Switching frictions and tenure

Ofcom documents contract lock-in, possible early-termination charges, lower
prices available through re-contracting/switching, and ongoing consumer
switching in communications markets.

Primary sources:

- Ofcom, *Are you in or out of contract?*, updated 17 July 2026:
  https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/saving-money/in-or-out
- Ofcom, *Pricing and consumer engagement 2025*, published 26 February 2026:
  https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/bills-and-charges/pricing-and-consumer-engagement
- Ofcom, 12 September 2025, One Touch Switch update:
  https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/switching-provider/1.6-million-brits-hit-switch-on-their-broadband-provider

Implication for GSS:
switching friction and customer tenure are empirically/institutionally
meaningful primitives.

Critical welfare limitation:
a contractual monetary exit fee may be partly a transfer to a firm, whereas the
GSS parameter `sigma` enters consumer utility as a real disutility/cost.
The exact P4 resource-loss interpretation therefore maps most directly to
time, hassle, learning, compatibility, or other real switching costs, not to a
purely redistributive fee.

## 8. Generality audit

### What genuinely generalizes without changing the theory

The certified logic applies to any literal relabeling of the same economic
structure:

- two positive-mass consumer cohorts;
- one cohort carries a one-sided switching/attachment cost;
- one firm can cohort-discriminate while its rival uses a common price;
- horizontal differentiation generates the same clipped affine shares;
- switching cost is a real utility/resource cost for welfare accounting.

Examples may include subscription, communications, insurance, banking, or
other customer markets **only to the extent those primitives are credible**.

### What does not generalize without new theory

The results are not certified for:

- arbitrary demand systems;
- nonlinear transportation costs;
- endogenous switching effort;
- monetary switching fees treated as transfers;
- endogenous information acquisition;
- both firms discriminating;
- imperfect recognition;
- dynamic customer acquisition/harvesting;
- mixed-strategy or outside-branch equilibria not characterized here;
- endogenous adoption of HBP;
- alternative equilibrium selections at weak boundaries.

Therefore "generality" is not a separate contribution. It is primarily
interpretive portability of a source-specific correction.

## 9. Testable / observable predictions inside the model

Conditional on moving from uniform pricing to the displayed HBP equilibrium:

1. incumbent old-customer price rises by `theta sigma/2`;
2. incumbent new-customer price falls by `(1-theta)sigma/2`;
3. entrant common price is unchanged;
4. incumbent loses old customers by `theta sigma/(4 tau)`;
5. incumbent gains new customers by `(1-theta)sigma/(4 tau)`;
6. aggregate incumbent market share is unchanged;
7. entrant profit is unchanged;
8. incumbent profit rises;
9. consumer surplus falls;
10. transportation cost falls but switching-cost loss rises by more;
11. net welfare falls on `D_compare`.

These are model predictions, not empirical findings.

The most distinctive observable signature is the joint combination:

> customer-composition reallocation with unchanged aggregate incumbent share
> and unchanged entrant price.

quadratic in `sigma` and share the same `theta(1-theta)` factor.

- incumbent profit: `+2/16`;
- entrant profit: `0`;
- total welfare: `-1/16`;

all multiplied by
`theta(1-theta)sigma^2/tau`.

A second resource row can show:

- transportation cost: `-3/16`;
- switching cost: `+4/16`;
- total resource cost: `+1/16`.

## 11. Stage-7 kill tests

### Welfare-is-only-transfer test

**PASS.**

The total-welfare difference equals a real-resource cost difference:
additional switching loss exceeds reduced transportation cost.

### Private/social wedge test

**PASS WITH SCOPE LIMIT.**

Incumbent profit and total welfare rank the regimes differently, but regime
choice is exogenous, so no adoption/policy theorem follows.

### Institutional-primitives test

**PASS WITH LIMITATIONS.**

Primary regulatory evidence supports history-based pricing capability,
existing/new customer differentiation, and switching frictions. It does not
validate the exact GSS information structure or treat every real-world
switching fee as a social cost.

### Generality test

**DOWNGRADED / NOT A CONTRIBUTION.**

The correction has credible applications beyond one named industry, but this is
mostly mapping of the same primitives. No broad general theorem is claimed.

### Visual-interest test

**PASS.**

The nested global-validity regions are genuinely informative and can be shown
without unverified computation. The welfare result is better shown in a
decomposition table than a decorative plot.

## 12. Maximum defensible contribution after Stage 7

> Gehrig, Shy & Stenbacka's displayed HBP and uniform-price formulas are
> globally valid only on explicitly characterized domains. On their common
> certified domain, correcting consumer surplus reverses the paper's welfare
> ranking. The reversal has a real-resource interpretation: HBP improves
> spatial matching but induces enough additional switching to more than offset
> that gain.

Prohibited stronger interpretation:

> History-based or personalized pricing is generally socially harmful and
> should be prohibited.
