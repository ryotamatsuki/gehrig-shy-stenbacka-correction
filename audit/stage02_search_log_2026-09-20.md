states that the incumbent's profit gain from history-based pricing exceeds the
associated loss to consumers. This confirms that the disputed welfare headline
survives at the publication-record level and is not merely an artifact of the
14 December 2009 author draft.

## Closest same-author comparison

Gehrig, Shy & Stenbacka (2012), "A Welfare Evaluation of History-Based Price
Discrimination," Journal of Industry, Competition and Trade 12, 373–393,
DOI 10.1007/s10842-011-0111-8.

This is a different asymmetric-duopoly game in which both firms can price
discriminate. It does not constitute an erratum or corrected version of the
2011 entrant-information model.

## Strongest forward-citation check

Hinnerk Gnutzmann (2014), "Price Discrimination in Asymmetric Industries:
Implications for Competition and Welfare," explicitly describes GSS (2011) as
a closely related model that constrains the entrant to uniform pricing and says
that allowing the entrant to discriminate reverses the GSS social-welfare
result.

Relevant open working-paper text:
https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/170616/1/disce-wp019.pdf

SSRN:
https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2490822

This is not absorption of the present correction. Gnutzmann changes the
entrant's strategy/information technology and obtains a different model result.
The correction project retains the original GSS strategy set and finds that the
GSS welfare headline reverses already within that same model after correcting
consumer surplus.

## Later same-author dynamic work

Oz Shy, Rune Stenbacka & David Hao Zhang (2016), "History-based versus uniform
pricing in growing and declining markets," International Journal of Industrial
Organization 48, 88–117, DOI 10.1016/j.ijindorg.2016.06.002.

This paper analyzes an infinite-horizon overlapping-generations model with
consumer lock-in and market growth/decline. It is a dynamic extension in a
different game, not a correction of the 2011 arithmetic or global-equilibrium
claim.

## Application-neutral parent-class search

Canonicalized source game:

> A firm chooses one common price against a weighted sum of two clipped affine
> demand segments with different horizontal shifts, while its rival can set
> segment-specific prices.

Searches covered:
- Hotelling global price equilibrium with corner/full-capture branches;
- piecewise-linear price competition;
- captive/loyal and switcher segments;
- common pricing across market segments;
- global best responses with clipped demands.

The literature contains related piecewise-price and captive-consumer models,
but no located theorem directly replaces the GSS correction by simultaneously
mapping to this exact asymmetric price-instrument structure, generating the
source-specific validity conditions, and correcting the welfare identity.

Therefore the active-set machinery is treated as method, not as a claimed new
general theorem.
